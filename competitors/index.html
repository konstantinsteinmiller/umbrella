Summary

Umbrella sits in a thin niche: wind as the puzzle, the umbrella as the only tool. Nobody found in this research ships exactly that on a big web portal. The nearest games are either premium indie titles that treat the umbrella as a multi-purpose action tool (Gunbrella, Emma's Umbrella, Moonbrella) or tiny itch.io prototypes (Windward, Bea & Brolly). Web portals reward adjacent "glide / one-input flow" games (paper-plane titles on CrazyGames and Poki).

The five things that matter most

  1. Fix the first 60 seconds before anything else. Money on web portals scales with ad impressions per session; sessions that end after 30–50 s cannot earn, whatever the traffic.
  2. Give the umbrella a second verb. Every successful umbrella game found uses it for more than float/close (dash, jab, swing, build platforms, dive for speed).
  3. Add a meta loop (unlockable umbrellas/skins, daily challenge, ghost races, stars → rewards). Umbrella already has time-stars, a level editor and community levels — the pieces exist but are not a loop yet.
  4. Treat web portals as the funnel, not the destination. The realistic upside is the same game as a paid Steam title (with the level editor as the selling point) and/or a hybrid-casual mobile build.
  5. Use Poki Playtest rounds deliberately (the new Playtest Build chapter does this): one change per round, measure % of players past 90 s.

Steam review counts (a rough proxy for sales)

Rule of thumb (Boxleiter-style, heuristic, not a measurement): units sold ≈ 30–50 × review count. For Gunbrella that gives ≈ 28–47 k, which matches the third-party estimate [2]. Emma's Umbrella would be ≈ 330–550 units; Ynglet ≈ 9–15 k.

Where Umbrella is different

  • Wind is the puzzle, not decoration — currents, super streams, buttons that change direction, gates (levels are authored around reading air).
  • Risk that you can feel — fall too far and you splat; opening at speed flips the umbrella inside out.
  • A built-in editor with share links and a community list — none of the umbrella games found ship that.
  • Runs everywhere — web portals, touch and desktop, 21 languages.

What competitors do better than Umbrella

Verb depth

Gunbrella (glide, swing, dash, dive) [1], Emma's Umbrella (float, dash, hang, build platforms, wall-jump) [3] and Moonbrella (jab, hook, float) [4] all use the umbrella as a toolkit. Umbrella has two states: open and closed.

A reason to come back

Papery Planes unlocks planes; Alto's has goals and moods [9][10]; Gunbrella has scrap → upgrades [1]. Umbrella has stars and a level list, but no progression that changes how it plays.

Instant, endless loop

The CrazyGames/Poki paper-plane games are "tap and fly" from second one, with score as the goal [9]. Umbrella's first 20–30 s are loader, tap and intro (own playtest report), then levels 4–5 are a wall.

Identity & hook line

"A gun that is also an umbrella" and "a floating non-platformer" [1][8] are one-sentence hooks. Umbrella's store text is accurate but generic ("one-button sky platformer").

Production & publisher muscle

Gunbrella was acquired and published by Devolver [2]; Ynglet by Akupara [8]. Umbrella is a solo web build with no marketing budget.

Discoverability tools

Moonbrella uses a public Steam demo [4]; Poki pushes new games it can measure fast. Umbrella's levels 1–3 are near-automatic, so a short demo does not yet show the "wind puzzle" hook.

Audiences Umbrella could target

No reliable age/gender data was found for any portal; the groups below are inferred from what the competing games attract.

1 · Portal casual players now

Short-session, desktop and mobile players on Poki/CrazyGames (Poki says 40 % mobile by its own count, Similarweb ≈ 32 % [12]). They reward instant fun and a visible next goal.

2 · Cozy / precision indie fans next

Steam players of Ynglet, Emma's Umbrella, Gris-like art games: willing to pay €2–15 for feel and art [3][8].

3 · Level creators & sharers next

People who like making and sending levels (Mario-Maker audience). Umbrella's share links make each creator a distribution channel.

4 · Speedrunners & streamers later

Time-stars, leaderboards and deterministic levels suit short speedrun videos.

5 · Families & school-break players later

One-button play and a calm tone suit unblocked-friendly portals; needs a clean content rating and no heavy ads.

6 · Mobile hybrid-casual needs UA budget

A native build with rewarded ads and IAP skins. Medians: D1 ≈ 22 %, D7 ≈ 3.4–3.9 % across mobile games [13].

Estimated playtime of one game (per session)

How these were derived: a CrazyGames visit of ≈ 7¾ min over ≈ 4 pages is ≈ 2 min per page, so a game that holds a player is plausibly 2–5 min there. Poki's 15 min visit is spread over several games, and Poki's own bar for a healthy game is 5+ min, so a typical decent Poki game likely sits around 3–6 min. These are inferences, not measurements: your own Poki Player Fit Test histogram (500 players) replaces them with the real thing.

Umbrella is roughly 6–10× below Poki's 5-minute bar. At about 40–60 s per level, reaching 5 minutes means a typical player must clear 5–7 levels in one sitting — today most leave around level 4–5.

How to win over the Poki audience

Poki decides with three measurements [14]: Playtest (recordings, the "why"), Player fit test (playtime histogram across 500 players, the "how much"), and Web fit test (CTR, time on page and conversion to play against category averages, ~10,000 players). Trade coverage adds that recommendations favour engagement such as average playtime and conversion over paid acquisition [11].

What Poki's audience rewards (traits of its current top games [15])

  • Easy to learn with no tutorial, tap/drag/swipe controls that work on desktop and phone, and sessions of seconds to a couple of minutes — Umbrella's one-button play already fits; the coach and first-win level close the gap.
  • Strong feedback on every action and instant restart — a close cousin on the Popular list is the short-level "rage" platformer Level Devil; Umbrella's splat can become that same "one more try" loop if restart is immediate and the cause of death is obvious.
  • Shareable moments (funny failures, high scores) — add a replay/ghost or a share link on the splat and win screens.
  • Retention hooks — progression, upgrades, unlocks, instant restarts [15].
  • Earned rewarded video — rewards that feel earned rather than interrupting give higher retention, and one Poki game earns up to €1 M a year from rewarded video alone [11].

How profitable could it become?

Assumption-driven. Every number below is Scenario = (inputs you can change) × (formula shown). Sourced facts: Poki's 50/50 split on Poki-sourced traffic and 100 % on your own traffic (one guide, unverified) [12]; CrazyGames "up to 55–60 %" (sources disagree) [12]; hybrid-casual ARPDAU $0.15–0.50 (vendor blog) [13]. No source gave eCPM or revenue per 1,000 plays for web portals — the inputs below are assumptions to be replaced with your Poki dashboard values.

A · Web portals (Poki / CrazyGames) — ad revenue

Monthly revenue = sessions × ad impressions per session × eCPM ÷ 1000 × developer share.

B · Steam (premium) — one-off sales

Net per unit ≈ price × 0.70 (Steam's cut) × ~0.85 (VAT, refunds, regional pricing) — an assumption. Units come from the review heuristic above.

Reference: Emma's Umbrella (€1.99, 11 reviews) ≈ a few hundred units [3]; Ynglet 296 reviews [8]; Gunbrella ≈ 28.9 k units / ≈ $311 k gross, third-party [2]. Publisher, art and marketing explain most of the gap between rows.

C · Mobile hybrid-casual

Monthly revenue = DAU × ARPDAU × 30. Organic only; paid acquisition cost per install was not found for this genre, so profit after UA is not modelled.

Realistic reading

  • Today: a funnel that loses most players at 30–50 s earns tens of dollars a month even with decent traffic (see the "Base" row).
  • Most plausible upside (12 months): fix the funnel, add a meta loop and rewarded hooks → low four figures per month on portals, plus a Steam release in the €5–25 k range if the editor lands.
  • Tail case: a portal feature or viral level-sharing could reach five figures per month — it is a lottery ticket made more likely by retention, not a plan.

What would move each scenario

  • Base → Funnel fixed: past-90-seconds rate (Poki Playtest A/B), first-win level, readable deaths, cover image CTR.
  • Funnel fixed → Portal feature: a retention that portals' algorithms reward (return visits, session length), then curation/featuring; both Poki and CrazyGames are said to promote games they can measure fast [11][12].
  • Spillover: every shared level link is free acquisition; track shared-level opens per player.

Risks & caveats

  • Niche size only a handful of umbrella games sell, and most have tiny review counts [3][5].
  • Crowding over 15,000 HTML5 games shipped in H1 2025, 2.7× H1 2024 [12].
  • Source quality market-size and visitor numbers vary by source (e.g. CrazyGames 35–90 M); revenue estimates for Gunbrella are third-party [2]; Windward's page could not be read automatically.
  • Direct rivals Moonbrella and Upbrella may reach Steam with the same pitch and more depth [4][5].
  • Revenue share terms differ by traffic source and deal; check the portal contracts [12].
